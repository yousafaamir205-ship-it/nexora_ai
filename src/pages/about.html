Three engineers, one recurring conversation

NEXORA grew out of a weekend study group into a full mentorship program. Here's the short version of how that happened, and who's still running it.

How we got here

  1. 21

    A study group, not a company

    Three junior-to-mid engineers started meeting on Saturdays to help friends debug their side projects. Twelve people showed up to the fourth session.

  2. 22

    First structured cohort

    The Saturday sessions became a scheduled eight-week program with actual project deadlines. Eighteen students finished the first cohort; eleven had job offers within four months.

  3. 23

    Backend & Databases track opens

    Alumni kept asking for something beyond frontend work, so we brought on a database engineer to build a second track around schema design and query performance.

  4. 25

    Cybersecurity Fundamentals, and a real mentor bench

    The mentor pool passed 100 people. A third track launched, focused on access control and secure defaults rather than certification exam prep.

  5. 26

    Cohort 14, today

    Still the same core idea as the Saturday sessions: an engineer who's done the job, sitting across from you while you learn to do it.

What we hold mentors to

"Good job" isn't a review comment. Mentors point at the exact line and explain what a reviewer at their company would flag and why.

Mentors volunteer their evenings. In exchange we ask for reliability — reschedule with notice rather than a no-show, same as any team would expect.

There's no such thing as a "dumb question" in a session. If a student is stuck on something fundamental, that's a signal to slow down, not to move on.
